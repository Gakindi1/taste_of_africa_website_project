Isombe

Rwanda's beloved stew of pounded cassava leaves, aubergine and peanuts, rich with red palm oil.

  • Total time2 h 30 min
  • Serves4
  • DifficultyEasy
Start cooking ↓

Ingredients

4
  • 500 g pounded cassava leaves (fresh or frozen)
  • 1 large aubergine, diced
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 cup spinach (optional)
  • ½ cup natural peanut butter or ground peanuts
  • 3 tbsp red palm oil
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 stock cube
  • Salt, to taste

About this dish

Isombe is one of Rwanda's most cherished home-cooked dishes, made from young cassava leaves that are pounded and simmered for hours until soft and silky. Similar dishes are loved across the region — sombe in Burundi, pondu in the Congo and kisamvu in Tanzania.

Vegetables like aubergine and peppers add body, while ground peanuts and red palm oil make the stew wonderfully rich. Isombe is often shared at family gatherings and served with ugali, rice, boiled plantain or sweet potatoes.

Region: East Africa

Cooking instructions

Tap each stage to expand or collapse the steps.

01Cook the cassava leaves
  1. Rinse the pounded cassava leaves well and place them in a large pot with the water.
  2. Boil uncovered for 1 to 1½ hours, topping up with water as needed.
  3. This long cooking softens the leaves and removes natural bitterness — cassava leaves must always be thoroughly cooked.
02Add the vegetables
  1. Add the aubergine, onion, spring onions, green pepper, garlic and spinach.
  2. Simmer for 20 minutes until the vegetables are soft.
03Enrich and finish
  1. Loosen the peanut butter with a ladle of hot cooking liquid, then stir it into the pot.
  2. Add the palm oil, crumbled stock cube and salt.
  3. Simmer for a final 15–20 minutes, stirring often, until thick and glossy.
  4. Serve hot with ugali, rice, boiled plantain or sweet potatoes.

Chef's tip: Frozen pounded cassava leaves from African grocery stores save a lot of time and work perfectly.
