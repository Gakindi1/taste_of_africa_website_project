Bunny Chow

Durban's legendary street food: a hollowed-out loaf of bread filled with rich, fiery lamb curry.

  • Total time2 h
  • Serves4
  • DifficultyMedium
Start cooking

Ingredients

4
  • 1 kg lamb or mutton, cubed
  • 1 large unsliced white loaf
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3 green cardamom pods
  • 1 tsp fennel seeds
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 3 tbsp Durban masala (hot curry powder)
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 2 tomatoes, grated
  • 3 potatoes, quartered
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 handful fresh coriander
  • Salt, to taste

About this dish

Bunny chow was born in Durban, South Africa, in the 1940s within the city's Indian community, descendants of workers who arrived in the 19th century to labour on the sugar plantations. Serving curry inside a hollowed loaf made it portable, affordable and easy to eat on the go, no plates needed.

Despite the name, there's no rabbit involved! A 'quarter' bunny is a quarter loaf filled with curry made with Durban masala, curry leaves and whole spices. The scooped-out bread, called the 'virgin', is placed back on top to mop up the gravy.

Region: Southern Africa

Cooking instructions

Tap each stage to expand or collapse the steps.

01Bloom the spices
  1. Heat the oil in a large pot and add the cinnamon, cardamom, fennel seeds and curry leaves.
  2. Fry for 1 minute until fragrant, then add the onions and cook until golden.
02Build the curry
  1. Stir in the ginger-garlic paste, Durban masala and turmeric for 1 minute (add a splash of water if it sticks).
  2. Add the lamb and stir until every piece is coated and lightly browned.
  3. Add the grated tomatoes, cover and cook on low heat for 45 minutes.
  4. Add the potatoes and water, and simmer for another 30–40 minutes until tender and the gravy is thick.
03Assemble the bunny
  1. Cut the loaf into quarters. Hollow out each piece, keeping the scooped-out bread (the 'virgin').
  2. Ladle the curry into the bread, sprinkle with coriander and place the virgin on top.
  3. Serve with a quick sambal of grated carrot, onion and chilli.

Chef's tip: Eat a bunny with your hands: tear off pieces of the bread walls and use them to scoop up the curry.
