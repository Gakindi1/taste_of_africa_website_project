Jollof Rice

The smoky, tomato-rich one-pot rice that sparks friendly 'Jollof Wars' across West Africa.

  • Total time1 h 15 min
  • Serves6
  • DifficultyMedium
Start cooking ↓

Ingredients

6
  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 4 ripe plum tomatoes
  • 2 red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 2 scotch bonnet peppers
  • 2 medium onions
  • ⅓ cup vegetable oil
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3 cups chicken stock
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 2 stock cubes
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • Salt, to taste

About this dish

Jollof rice is West Africa's celebration dish — no wedding, birthday or Sunday lunch is complete without a steaming pot. Its roots trace back to the Wolof people of the old Jolof Empire in present-day Senegal and The Gambia, whose rice dish thieboudienne inspired versions across the region.

Today Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal and Sierra Leone each cook it their own way, and the playful rivalry over who makes the best is known as the 'Jollof Wars'. The Nigerian style below gets its colour from a reduced base of tomatoes, red bell peppers and fiery scotch bonnet, and its famous smoky flavour from the slightly toasted rice at the bottom of the pot — the prized party jollof taste.

Region: West Africa

Cooking instructions

Tap each stage to expand or collapse the steps.

01Prepare the pepper base
  1. Roughly chop the tomatoes, bell peppers, scotch bonnets and one onion, then blend until smooth.
  2. Pour the blend into a pot and boil uncovered for 15–20 minutes until reduced by about half.
  3. Rinse the rice in cold water two or three times until the water runs clear, then drain.
02Fry and season
  1. Heat the oil in a heavy-based pot and fry the second onion, sliced, until soft and translucent.
  2. Stir in the tomato paste and fry for 5 minutes until it darkens to a deep red.
  3. Add the reduced pepper base, thyme, curry powder, bay leaves and crumbled stock cubes.
  4. Fry for 10–15 minutes, stirring often, until the oil rises to the surface.
03Steam the rice
  1. Pour in the stock, season with salt, then add the rice and stir once to combine.
  2. Cover the pot with foil and a tight lid, and cook on very low heat for 30 minutes.
  3. Avoid stirring — the steam does the work. Add a splash of stock if it looks dry.
04Finish and serve
  1. Stir in the butter. For smoky 'party jollof', turn up the heat for 3–5 minutes so the bottom lightly toasts.
  2. Fluff with a fork and serve with fried plantain, grilled chicken and coleslaw.

Chef's tip: Use parboiled long-grain rice — it holds its shape and won't turn mushy during the long steam.
