Injera

A tangy, spongy fermented flatbread that serves as plate, spoon and heart of every Ethiopian meal.

  • Total time3 days + 30 min
  • Serves6
  • DifficultyHard
Start cooking ↓

Ingredients

6
  • 2 cups teff flour
  • 3 cups lukewarm filtered water
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp active dry yeast (optional, speeds up fermentation)
  • A little oil, for the pan

About this dish

Injera is made from teff, a tiny, iron-rich, naturally gluten-free grain that has been grown in the Ethiopian and Eritrean highlands for thousands of years. The batter is left to ferment for several days, giving the bread its gentle sourness and the thousands of little holes — called 'eyes' — on its surface.

Injera is the plate, the spoon and the bread all at once. Colourful stews such as berbere-spiced doro wat (chicken) and misir wat (red lentils) are spooned on top, and diners tear off pieces to scoop them up. Feeding a friend a bite by hand, called gursha, is a gesture of love and respect.

Region: East Africa

Cooking instructions

Tap each stage to expand or collapse the steps.

01Ferment the batter (2–3 days)
  1. Whisk the teff flour (and yeast, if using) with 2½ cups of water into a smooth, thin batter.
  2. Cover loosely with a clean cloth and leave at room temperature for 2–3 days.
  3. It's ready when it smells pleasantly sour and bubbles appear. Pour off any liquid that settles on top.
02Prepare to cook
  1. Stir in the salt and enough of the remaining water to reach a thin pancake-batter consistency.
  2. Leave for 30 minutes until bubbles form again on the surface.
03Cook the injera
  1. Heat a large non-stick pan or traditional mitad over medium-high heat and wipe with a little oil.
  2. Pour the batter in a thin spiral from the outside in, covering the whole pan.
  3. When holes appear across the surface, cover with a lid for about 1 minute — do not flip!
  4. The injera is done when the edges lift away. Slide it onto a clean cloth to cool before stacking.
04Serve
  1. Line a large platter with injera and spoon stews such as doro wat, misir wat and shiro on top.
  2. Roll extra injera on the side for scooping, and eat with your right hand.

Chef's tip: Fermentation is faster in a warm kitchen. Use filtered water — chlorine in tap water can slow the natural yeasts.
